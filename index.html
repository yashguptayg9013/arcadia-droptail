I  —  Heaven on Earth

The ancient Greeks imagined Arcadia as heaven on earth: untouched nature, held in perfect harmony. Its owner asked Rolls-Royce for exactly that. A calm, tactile refuge from a life spent doing business.

2019

True to the

The client fell for Droptail as a single hand-drawn profile: the low stance, the cabin that wraps around you, the long falling body line. Their one condition was that the finished car never stray from that drawing.

III  —  Form

Let the shape
do the talking.

Droptail is the first roadster body style in Rolls-Royce's modern history. Where other commissions add, Arcadia subtracts: muted colour, clean monolithic panels and bold negative sculpture that sunlight finds on its own.

IV  —  Light

A white that
never ends.

At first glance, a solid white. Then the sun finds it. The paint is laced with aluminium and glass particles, so it shimmers and seems to go on forever in depth. The client chose a silver with coarser flakes to contrast with it, in tone and in intensity.

  • Bespoke WhiteSolid white with aluminium and glass particles
  • Bespoke SilverLarger aluminium flake, for a faceted metallic
  • BrightworkGrille, vanes and wheels, mirror-polished

Luxury, said quietly.

V  —  Wood

Everything begins
with the grain.

The client sent reference after reference, from homes and offices to classic cars, to pin down a texture, a grain and a colour. The answer was Santos Straight Grain: a dense hardwood with an interlocking pattern and the finest grain of any species Rolls-Royce uses. Machine it carelessly and it tears. Dry it too fast and it cracks.

  • 233pieces of wood across the car
  • 76on the rear deck alone
  • 55°the angle every grain is laid at
  • 8,000hof wood and coating development
  • 1,000hof simulated world weather, on 18 samples
  • 1coat of lacquer, for the car's lifetime

The car would live partly in the tropics. Superyacht coatings were ruled out because they need re-applying. Instead, Rolls-Royce developed a lacquer that goes on once, for life, and proved it with 1,000 hours of spray, darkness, heat and glare in a machine that cycles the world's weather.

VI  —  A study in white

01

Two colours, named for one person

White leather carries the coachwork inside; a tan was developed to sit perfectly against the wood. Both hues are named after the client and reserved for them alone.

02

The shawl

The largest continuous piece of wood ever put in a Rolls-Royce. It reads as two mirrored veneers; it is actually 40 sections, each mapped digitally and laid at 55° to match the deck.

03

A floating plinth

Wood this delicate cannot flex. The dashboard, door linings and cantilevered armrest sit on a carbon-fibre base layered with Formula 1 techniques, rigid at any speed.

04

Made for the world

Left-hand drive, for use across continents. Before a panel was made, the client toured the car through favourite cities in Rolls-Royce's virtual-reality "holodeck".

VII  —  Timepiece

The most complex clock
Rolls-Royce has made.

Over two years of development, then five months just to assemble it. The raw-metal guilloché carries 119 facets for the year the client first saw the car: 2023, the marque's 119th.

facets of geometric guilloché in raw metal

VIII  —  Four years

Four years.
One person.

Designers spent months studying the client's world, from clothes and furniture to food and travel, until they could describe a taste the client hadn't put into words. Even the family was shown the final design. It turned out to be more modern than anyone expected.

  1. 2019

    The client is shown Droptail as one hand-drawn sketch and asks that it never change.

  2. Months

    Designers decode a personal aesthetic: lightness, natural materials, precision.

  3. Holodeck

    Through a VR headset, the client sees the car in the places they will drive it.

  4. 8,000 h

    Wood and lacquer are developed, and the 1,000-hour weather tests run.

  5. Late 2023

    First preview of the car, in the marque's 119th year.

  6. Feb 2024

    Handed over at a private ceremony in Singapore.
